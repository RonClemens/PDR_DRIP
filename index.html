What is the DRIP? DI-SESS-81757A

The Design Review Information Package is a government-directed Data Item Description (DID) requiring the contractor to assemble and deliver a structured package of design artifacts at each formal design review milestone. Per DI-SESS-81757A, the DRIP enables the Government to:

  • ▸
    Determine that the emerging system has demonstrated sufficient maturity to enter the next phase of development
  • ▸
    Verify technical baselines (functional → allocated) are properly established and controlled
  • ▸
    Assess risks, trade studies, and design decisions against validated capability requirements
  • ▸
    Support the Milestone B certification required by 10 U.S.C. § 2366b for MDAPs
Design Review Progression
SRR — System Requirements Review
Establishes system mission, operational views, DRM scenarios, architecture boundaries, and internal structure. First section of the DRIP.
PDR — Preliminary Design Review ★ THIS MODULE
Establishes the allocated baseline. All functional and interface requirements decomposed to CI level. Preliminary hardware and software designs complete. Verifies design satisfies the CDD within cost and schedule.
CDR — Critical Design Review
Confirms detailed design is complete and ready for fabrication, integration, and test. Establishes product baseline.

PDR Section Content Requirements
DI-SESS-81757A · PDR DRIP Content Specification
Customer Perspective: As the Government representative, you are evaluating whether the contractor's DRIP PDR section is complete, accurate, and sufficient to authorize proceeding to detailed design. Each element below must be addressed by the contractor and reviewed by you.
Required PDR DRIP Content Elements Mandatory
  • a
    System Performance Specification Status. Documented evidence that the system performance specification satisfies the validated CDD. All KPPs, KSAs, and TPMs must be allocated and traceable.
  • b
    Preliminary Hardware & Software Design. Complete preliminary design for all hardware and software configuration items. Includes system architecture, block diagrams, and design descriptions sufficient to enter detailed design.
  • c
    Interface Requirements & Control Documents. All internal and external interface requirements documented in Interface Control Documents (ICDs). All external interfaces from SRR fully addressed. Internal CI-to-CI interfaces defined.
  • d
    Software Architecture & CSCI Identification. All Computer Software Configuration Items (CSCIs), Computer Software Components (CSCs), and Computer Software Units (CSUs) defined. Software Requirements Specifications (SRSs) baselined for all CSCs.
  • e
    Trade Study Results. Results of all significant trade studies conducted during preliminary design. Documentation of design decisions, options considered, and rationale. Must include producibility, affordability, and sustainment trades.
  • f
    Risk Assessment & Mitigation Plans. Updated risk register with all identified technical, schedule, and cost risks. Mitigation strategies and status. ESOH risk identification. Unique software risks addressed with mitigation plans.
  • g
    Reliability & Maintainability Analysis. Updated R&M estimates based on engineering analyses, testing, or modeling. FMECA completion status. Built-in-test, fault detection, and false alarm rate derivations.
  • h
    Manufacturing & Producibility Assessment. Producibility assessments of key technologies. Majority of manufacturing processes defined, characterized, and documented. Preliminary production cost assessment within production budget.
  • i
    Logistics & Sustainment Planning. Life Cycle Sustainment Plan (LCSP) updated and approved. Sustainment requirements allocated to CIs with traceability to the RAM-C Rationale and CDD. Long-lead items identified.
  • j
    Schedule & Program Plans. Updated Integrated Master Schedule (IMS) showing critical path to CDR. Program staffing verified. Software development schedules reflect contractor processes.
  • k
    Cost Analysis Requirements Description (CARD). Updated CARD consistent with the proposed allocated baseline. System cost model updated and allocated to lower system element levels.

Allocated Baseline
PDR's Primary Technical Deliverable
Key Customer Understanding: The Preliminary Design Review does one thing above all else—it establishes the allocated baseline. This baseline is placed under formal configuration control at PDR. As the customer, you must verify it is complete before approving PDR exit.
What Constitutes a Complete Allocated Baseline?
  • 1
    All system-level functional and interface requirements have been decomposed and allocated to the lowest level of the specification tree for all system elements (Configuration Item level)
  • 2
    All external interfaces addressed at SRR are documented in Interface Control Documents (ICDs)
  • 3
    All internal interfaces (system element to system element) are documented in ICDs
  • 4
    Verification requirements to demonstrate achievement of all specified allocated performance characteristics have been documented
  • 5
    Design constraints have been captured and incorporated into the requirements and design documentation
Functional Baseline (Prior) SRR/SDR

Established at earlier reviews. Describes what the system must do at the system level.

System Performance Spec └── System-level requirements └── KPPs, KSAs, TPMs └── External interfaces
Allocated Baseline (PDR Output) ★ PDR

Established at PDR. Decomposes requirements to CI level for both hardware and software.

Development Specifications ├── HW CI Specs (per CI) │ ├── Functional reqs │ └── Verification plans └── SW CI Specs (SRS/IRS) ├── CSCI → CSC → CSU └── SW interface ICDs
Baseline Traceability — Customer Verification Points
Traceability Path Government Action Status Indicator
CDD → System Performance Spec Verify all KPP/KSA thresholds appear in spec Validate at PDR entry
System Spec → CI Dev Specs Spot-check requirement allocation completeness Audit allocated baseline
CI Specs → ICDs Confirm all interfaces addressed internally and externally ICD review team
CI Specs → Verification Plans Verify each requirement has a verification method (T/I/A/D) TEMP cross-reference
Functional Baseline → Allocated Baseline Confirm traceability is complete and consistent Risk if gaps found
Critical Point: After PDR, the allocated baseline goes under formal configuration management. Any changes require an Engineering Change Proposal (ECP) and Configuration Control Board (CCB) action. Your role as customer includes ensuring CCB governance is activated.

PDR Entry & Exit Criteria
Customer Gatekeeping Responsibilities
Your Authority: The Government Technical Review Chair determines when PDR may begin (entry) and when it is complete (exit). These decisions are yours—not the contractor's. Criteria must be documented in the Systems Engineering Plan (SEP) no later than Milestone A.
Entry Criteria Before PDR Begins

Do not allow PDR to start unless:

  • ✓
    All prior technical review action items are closed
  • ✓
    PDR criteria are documented in the SEP
  • ✓
    Validated CDD is in place (CJCSI 3170.01)
  • ✓
    System Performance Specification is complete
  • ✓
    All SOW/contract entry criteria satisfied
  • ✓
    DRIP package delivered with sufficient lead time
  • ✓
    Subject matter experts identified and invited
Exit Criteria After PDR Approval

PDR is complete only when:

  • ✓
    Allocated baseline established and under CM
  • ✓
    Technical data complete and satisfies sys perf spec
  • ✓
    Risks assessed and at acceptable level
  • ✓
    Feasibility/cost/schedule within risk margins
  • ✓
    IMS updated with CDR critical path
  • ✓
    Corrective action plans completed
  • ✓
    CARD updated to reflect allocated baseline

Contract Data Requirements
CDRL / DD Form 1423 Obligations at PDR
CDRL Authority: The DRIP is ordered via DD Form 1423 on the Contract Data Requirements List (CDRL). The Statement of Work (SOW) establishes the scope of design effort; the CDRL establishes your contractual right to receive the DRIP package and associated artifacts. Both documents are required.

Roles & Responsibilities
Government and Contractor Accountability at PDR
Customer Focus: Every role below is a Government accountability unless marked Contractor. Know your lane — and know what to demand from the other side of the table.
Program Manager (PM) — Government Accountable
  • PM
    Fund & staff the PDR. Approve, resource, and schedule the system PDR as planned in the SEP. Ensure SMEs from engineering, T&E, logistics, and contracting participate in every review element.
  • PM
    Establish the path to CDR. Document the post-PDR plan in contract vehicles — SEMP, IMS, IMP — so the contractor has a contractually binding roadmap to detailed design.
  • PM
    Control Government-held baselines. Convene CCBs when changes to the allocated baseline are warranted. Enforce ECP discipline from PDR exit onward. No informal changes.
  • PM
    MDAP obligation. For ACAT ID programs, invite DASD(SE) and the CAE to PDR and make all review artifacts available to them in advance of the review.
  • PM
    Sign the PDR completion memo. Formally document PDR entry/exit decisions, open action item status, and conditional approval conditions in a written PM determination.
Lead Systems Engineer — Government Technical Authority
  • SE
    Own the PDR plan. Develop and execute the system PDR plan with quantifiable entry and exit criteria documented in the SEP no later than Milestone A.
  • SE
    Gate the review. Verify all pre-established entry criteria are met before allowing PDR to commence. Do not waive entry criteria without PM approval and documented rationale.
  • SE
    Assess allocated baseline completeness. Verify requirements are decomposed to CI level, all ICDs are present, and verification requirements (T/I/A/D) are documented for every allocated performance characteristic.
  • SE
    Evaluate design considerations. Confirm R&M, ESOH, corrosion prevention, HSI, cybersecurity, and IMD plans are addressed and documented in the DRIP package.
  • SE
    Update risk posture. Review risk register; ensure all identified risks have mitigation plans with owners and closure dates. Update risk, issue, and opportunity plans post-PDR.
  • SE
    Document the closure plan. Write the post-PDR closure plan in the SEP. Track all action items to closure with defined due dates and responsible parties.
Chief Developmental Tester T&E
  • T
    Participate in PDR and provide T&E analysis and assessments to date
  • T
    Verify TEMP is updated and T&E resources are aligned with the IMS critical path to CDR
  • T
    Review software metrics, test lab establishment status, and CI-level test approach
  • T
    Flag verification gaps — missing test methods for allocated requirements before PDR exit
DASD(SE) — MDAPs Only ACAT ID/IC
  • D
    Conduct independent PDR assessment and report findings to the Milestone Decision Authority
  • D
    Assess conduct and adequacy of PDR: stakeholder participation, open RFIs, action item volume and risk
  • D
    Report on allocated baseline establishment, CM activation, and CDRL delivery status
  • D
    ACAT IC programs: CAE conducts the independent assessment in place of DASD(SE)
Contracting Officer (KO) Contract Authority
  • KO
    CDRL compliance. Ensure DD Form 1423 line items for the DRIP and all PDR-associated deliverables (SRS, ICD, SDP, LCSP, TEMP) are properly cited and trigger dates enforced.
  • KO
    SOW enforcement. Confirm PDR event-driven milestones in the SOW are met. Issue cure notices or show-cause letters if contractor fails to deliver DRIP package ≥30 days prior to PDR.
  • KO
    Formal approvals. Process Government written approvals, conditional approvals, or rejections of CDRL deliverables within contractually required timelines. Silence is not approval.
Contractor Responsibilities — What You Should Demand Verify
Contractor Action Timing Government Verification
Deliver complete DRIP PDR section for Government pre-read ≥30 days before PDR KO tracks against CDRL due date
Present preliminary design for all subsystems and CIs At PDR SE verifies coverage against CI list
Demonstrate allocated baseline completeness At PDR SE spot-checks CDD to CI spec traceability
Respond to all Government RFI/RFAs in writing Per SOW schedule SE/KO track open RFI log to closure
Close all PDR action items per agreed schedule Post-PDR IMS PM monitors monthly; SE validates closure
Baseline all required CDRLs — SRS, ICD, SDP, LCSP At/by PDR exit KO issues formal written approval per CDRL
Update IMS with critical path to CDR At PDR exit PM and SE review IMS for realism and risk
Key Principle: The Government does not just attend PDR — it chairs it, gates it, and closes it. The contractor presents; the Government decides. If the allocated baseline is not complete, PDR exit is not approved — regardless of schedule pressure.
